The big idea

AI that plans for you teaches you nothing.

Every module in TeachAI is anchored to a quote from her own plan, teaches a named prompting move, and hands her a complete prompt already filled in with her topic and grade — which she can run live and watch work. The lesson plan gets better, and so does she.

User journey

One conversation, two paths

Everything happens in a single chat transcript — no dashboards, no menus. The fork: does Melissa have a lesson plan to show us?

3
Path A · she has a plan

Attach & analyze

"I want to read the real thing, not a description of it."

She attaches her plan (PDF or plain text). Claude reads it and returns a diagnosis in three parts — every claim anchored to a quote from her document:

  • What I read — the plan reflected back, plus what genuinely works (she keeps what's good)
  • Where it is actually weak — each weakness with the exact quote as evidence, why it matters, and who it loses ("this scaffold quietly removes the math for your students who need it most")
  • A training session — one module per weakness, ready to work through

While the minute-long analysis runs, the working label walks forward — "Finding where the thinking actually happens..." · "Checking which scaffolds quietly remove the math..." — so the wait reads as care, not lag.

4
Path A · the heart of the product

The training session

One module per weakness — each teaches a named prompting move

Every module gives her the move's name, why it fixes her weakness, and a complete prompt already filled in with her topic and grade. Run it here executes it live so she watches the move work before she's asked to believe in it.

5
Path A · the payoff

Rebuild my lesson

"Put the moves together and see your lesson rebuilt."

One button applies every move at once. Claude rebuilds the full lesson — big idea, the questions she'll ask, scaffolds that keep the demand high, phase timing, exit ticket — and she downloads it. It's visibly her lesson, upgraded by moves she just practiced.

3b
Path B · no plan to share

The intake interview

Modeling the most important lesson: context in, quality out

No plan? Claude asks four questions that would change what the lesson looks like — not a form for form's sake. Grade and class makeup, where students actually are, what's gone wrong before, constraints. Then it builds a complete plan from her answers. The meta-lesson lands either way: good AI output starts with good context.

6

Done — but repeatable

Both paths end on a downloadable lesson plan

The composer stays open: "What else do you want to work on?" The real success metric isn't this Thursday's lesson — it's whether Melissa opens a blank Claude chat next week and uses the moves on her own.

Under the hood

How it's built

React 19 + Vite + Tailwind v4, calling the Claude API (claude-opus-5) directly from the browser for the demo.

src/lib/claude.ts

Coach persona and every API call. Conversation streams at low effort; analysis and plan-building run non-streamed at high effort.

src/lib/schemas.ts

Structured outputs — the analysis (strengths, evidence-anchored weaknesses, training modules) and the full lesson plan come back as validated JSON, never parsed prose.

src/App.tsx

A phase machine (landing → coaching → choosing → analyzing → training → building → done) rendering one continuous transcript.

src/components/

TrainingWorkspace (modules with Run-it-here), IntakePanel (path B questions), PlanCard (the downloadable rebuild).
